# Filming the Gendered Body: Beginning to Visualize Paul Thomas Anderson's Filmography as It Technically Depicts Gender

My mini project was created as a way to start thinking about the analytical potential of the PTAnnotated project's metadata, specifically the alt-text for each screenshot. While not driven by an incredibly clear vision, my goal here was to breech the massive subject of gender across the films of his I have seen (sans *One Battle After Another*, as the screenshotting process is still in progress). My first idea was to compare gender breakdowns by the shot location -- whether the screenshot was labeled "interior" or "exterior." This concept was based in my personal interest in ecofeminism, queer ecology, and the general relationship between gender and space (domestic vs public, wild vs man-made, etc). As an extension of this idea, I began also comparing the gender breakdowns of tracking shots. The general goal remains the same here: experiment with the data to see how gender is being shot, being captured, in the films. Including movement as part of my analysis is conceptually based in the thesis of a Classics Colloquium I attended in Freshman year titled "Allusion as Alluvium: Reading Helen's Liquid Commons," which, very basically, was interrogating how Helen -- in her story and imaginary existence -- moved, and consequently how allusion can be read as a geological process (which got into some ecofeminist ideas as well, but I digress). 

The products of this project are graphs for *There Will Be Blood*, *Punch-Drunk Love*, and *Boogie Nights* -- all of which were governed by slightly different approaches. I went about the categorization of the gender breakdowns differently as I progressed, partially due to evolving methodology ideas and partially based on what I knew about each film. I purposefully chose to focus on films I had seen because I wanted to know the broader context beyond just the alt-text descriptions, even though I was just using the alt-text for the data itself.

## *Note on the "Gender Breakdowns" and the Limitations of my Data*

The extraction process for compiling the data on gender from the alt-text was incredibly subjective and inconsistent; this is because I a) made some singular decisions on particular alt-text descriptions and b) ran into trouble with my OpenRefine experimentation to automate the process. Each method of categorizing the gender breakdowns comes with its own faults and ommissions. The largest, most universal of the issues is the fact that a lot of the alt-text left gender breakdowns unspecified for group scenes. Though a screenshot was labeled with "group of people," etc, it could have been depicting only men. Off the top of my head, I believe this is most likely the case for all the films -- especially with screenshots of the men in their work settings. Therefore, the gender discrepancies are most likely even larger than my data suggests. However, the opposite is, of course, true as well: group scenes with a lot of women (such as shots of people at the parties in *Boogie Nights*) also did not make it into my dataset. Ultimately, my data as it stands currently is less about the capturing of gender in the films themselves as it is in the metadata-creating process from our own team. However, I would argue this does not negate the impact of the results; the alt-text descriptions are simply one way to interpret the films, and all film analysis is heavily based in personal interpretation.

In some ways, bizarely, that almost complicated my mindset to the point of simplification; I chose to start my categorization by simply labeling if a screenshot's alt-text mentioned a male figure and/or a female figure -- though, of course, the same issue of unspecified groups arose.

Alongside my visualizations, I also calculated some percentage information from the gender+location data in excel to draw out some semblance of a "conclusion" in a different format:

- Percentage Exterior "Female" Screenshots: 18%
- Percentage Exterior "Male" Screenshots: 38%

These numbers help show the unequal distribution of exterior vs interior shots within the already unequal division of gender. 

Note: my method of categorization grouped together people across ages in such a way that the significance of teenage sexual manipulation and exploitation was ignored. If the project were to be expanded, I would want to go back to my OpenRefine process and compile new data based on the mentions of the words "teenager," "boy," and "girl," as well as specific mentions of nudity and/or sexual acts. This would require a larger re-thinking of my categorization, as well -- specifications for screenshots that mention children and adults, and more thought put into how the shift from a character being a child to being an adult changes their role in the film and their level of agency (if it ever really does).
